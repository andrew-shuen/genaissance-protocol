Understanding CDH1-related disease

Patient summary

CDH1 is a tumour-suppressor gene. A pathogenic variant can raise the risk of diffuse gastric cancer and, in women, lobular breast cancer. These cancers can be difficult to see on routine imaging or endoscopy. Risk is not identical in every family, so I use the exact variant, pathology, and family history to guide decisions.

Biology. CDH1 encodes E-cadherin, a calcium-dependent cell-adhesion protein. Loss of E-cadherin disrupts epithelial cohesion and signalling. Tumorigenesis usually requires somatic inactivation of the remaining normal allele. Gastric precursor foci may be microscopic, multifocal, and distributed beneath an apparently normal mucosal surface.

Phenotypes that must be separated.

  • Classic CDH1-related hereditary diffuse gastric cancer (HDGC): pathogenic or likely pathogenic variant with diffuse gastric cancer in the family.
  • Hereditary lobular breast cancer-predominant CDH1: pathogenic variant with lobular breast cancer clustering but little or no diffuse gastric cancer.
  • Incidentally identified CDH1 pathogenic variant: found through broad panel or secondary testing with limited phenotype information.
  • HDGC-like, gene-negative family: clinical family criteria are met but no pathogenic CDH1 or CTNNA1 variant is found.
  • CDH1 variant of uncertain significance: should not drive gastrectomy or high-risk surveillance by itself.

Autosomal dominant inheritance means each child of a heterozygous carrier has a 50% chance of inheriting the familial variant. Penetrance is incomplete and strongly affected by ascertainment and family history.

How this pathway works

Cells in the stomach and breast lining hold on to each other with a “cell glue” protein called E-cadherin (made by CDH1), which is anchored inside the cell by α-catenin (made by CTNNA1). When this glue is lost, cells loosen and can spread one by one through the tissue, which is how diffuse gastric cancer and lobular breast cancer grow.

CDH1: CDH1 makes E-cadherin itself, the glue between neighbouring cells.

Sources: Guilford P, et al. Nature 1998;392:402–5 (PubMed).

Surveillance and prevention by subtype

Patient summary

The safest plan depends on the subtype. For a clearly pathogenic CDH1 variant in a classic HDGC family, preventive total gastrectomy remains the most reliable way to prevent gastric cancer. Expert endoscopy is an alternative when surgery is delayed or declined, but it can miss microscopic disease.

A. Classic HDGC phenotype with a CDH1 pathogenic variant

High gastric concern
  • RoutineRefer to an expert multidisciplinary HDGC centre after result confirmation.
  • RoutineBaseline high-definition upper endoscopy before surgery, with at least 30 minutes of inspection, targeted biopsies, and systematic non-targeted biopsies using an expert protocol.
  • RoutineDiscuss prophylactic total gastrectomy in early adulthood, commonly between ages 20 and 30, individualized to family history, maturity, health, and patient values.
  • RoutineIf gastrectomy is deferred or declined: expert endoscopic surveillance every 6-12 months. A 12-month interval is common; shorter intervals may be used after concerning findings.
  • RoutineTest for Helicobacter pylori and eradicate if present. Confirm eradication.
  • Not recommendedRoutine CT, PET, serum tumour markers, or standard endoscopy without systematic biopsies for screening an asymptomatic carrier.

B. Breast-predominant or incidental CDH1 pathogenic variant

Gastric risk uncertain
  • RoutineExpert pathology review of reported gastric and breast cancers in the pedigree and careful three-generation risk assessment.
  • RoutineBaseline expert endoscopy with systematic biopsies, generally from age 18 or at diagnosis in adults.
  • IndividualizeAnnual or 6-12 monthly expert endoscopy while gastric-risk clarification is ongoing. After repeated negative examinations, interval extension may be considered only by an HDGC expert team.
  • IndividualizeProphylactic gastrectomy remains an option for a confirmed pathogenic variant, but the balance is more preference-sensitive when there is no DGC family history.
  • Not recommendedGastrectomy for a VUS or a likely benign variant.

C. HDGC-like family, no pathogenic CDH1 or CTNNA1 variant

Family-based
  • RoutineConfirm diffuse gastric and lobular breast histology where possible and reconsider updated multigene testing.
  • RoutineAnnual expert endoscopy for at least 2 years, starting at age 40 or 10 years before the earliest gastric cancer in the family, but not before age 18.
  • IndividualizeAfter two negative examinations, extend the interval based on family history and specialist judgment.
  • Usually not recommendedProphylactic gastrectomy when no pathogenic variant is found and expert endoscopies are negative, unless exceptional pedigree or pathology features justify it.

D. CDH1 variant of uncertain significance

Do not treat as positive
  • RoutineManage using personal and family history, not the VUS.
  • RoutineRequest periodic laboratory reinterpretation and pursue segregation or RNA studies only when clinically appropriate.
  • Not recommendedPredictive testing of unaffected relatives solely for clinical decision-making, prophylactic gastrectomy, or risk-reducing mastectomy based only on the VUS.

Breast surveillance for women with a confirmed CDH1 P/LP variant

  • RoutineBreast awareness from age 20 and clinical assessment every 6-12 months in a high-risk clinic.
  • RoutineAnnual contrast-enhanced breast MRI from age 30.
  • RoutineAdd annual mammography from age 30-40 according to local guideline and breast density; many programs begin at 30 or 40 and stagger it 6 months from MRI.
  • OptionalUltrasound when MRI is unavailable or contraindicated. It is not a substitute for MRI when MRI can be performed.
  • Not recommendedMammography alone as the preferred high-risk strategy for lobular cancer.

Other organs

There is no established CDH1-specific screening for colorectal, ovarian, pancreatic, prostate, thyroid, or other cancers. Follow population screening and additional family-history-based recommendations.

Risk-reducing surgery and postoperative care

Patient summary

Removing the entire stomach prevents gastric cancer but creates permanent nutritional and digestive changes. This decision needs expert surgery, dietetics, genetics, gastroenterology, psychology, and pathology support. Breast surgery is optional and depends on personal preferences and breast-risk assessment.

Prophylactic total gastrectomy

Use an experienced upper-GI surgeon and a centre with HDGC pathology expertise. The operation must remove all gastric mucosa, with confirmation of oesophageal and duodenal margins. Routine extensive lymph-node dissection is not required for a truly prophylactic procedure with no invasive lesion, but operative planning changes if cancer is suspected.

Trade-offs: operative mortality is low in expert centres, but morbidity and lifelong effects are expected. Common issues include 10%-20% weight loss, early or late dumping, altered eating pattern, diarrhoea, reflux, micronutrient deficiency, bone loss, gallstones, and kidney stones.

Long-term monitoring: dietitian follow-up; small frequent meals; lifelong vitamin B12 replacement; periodic CBC, ferritin/iron studies, folate, calcium, vitamin D, parathyroid hormone, magnesium, albumin, liver and renal measures; bone-density assessment according to baseline status and local practice. Review oral drug absorption. Non-oral contraception may be preferable when reliable absorption is essential. Delay pregnancy about 6-12 months after surgery to allow nutritional recovery and weight stabilization.

Breast risk-reducing surgery

Bilateral risk-reducing mastectomy may be considered, but it is not mandatory. Evidence is extrapolated from other high-risk genes and there is no CDH1-specific survival trial. Discuss MRI-based surveillance, reconstructive options, age, comorbidity, and family experience. Risk-reducing salpingo-oophorectomy is not indicated for CDH1 alone.

Chemoprevention and medical prevention

Patient summary

There is no medicine proven to prevent CDH1-related stomach cancer. For breast risk, tamoxifen or other endocrine prevention may help some women, but the evidence is not specific to CDH1. We weigh clotting, uterine, bone, and menopausal risks before prescribing.

Gastric: no validated chemoprevention. Eradicate H. pylori, avoid tobacco, and investigate persistent upper-GI symptoms promptly.

Breast endocrine prevention in Canada: options used in high-risk care include tamoxifen 20 mg orally daily for 5 years for pre- or postmenopausal women; raloxifene 60 mg orally daily for 5 years for postmenopausal women; or an aromatase inhibitor such as anastrozole 1 mg orally daily for 5 years in selected postmenopausal women. Low-dose tamoxifen 5 mg daily is used in some settings but is not the standard CDH1-specific regimen.

Genetic counselling and family planning

Patient summary

Close relatives should be offered counselling and testing for the known family variant. Testing usually starts when adult management could change. Reproductive options exist, but choosing among them is personal.

  • Construct a three-generation pedigree and verify diffuse gastric versus intestinal gastric histology and lobular versus ductal breast histology.
  • Offer targeted cascade testing to adult first-degree relatives. Each has a 50% chance of carrying the familial variant.
  • Testing is generally offered from age 16-18 or legal adulthood, with earlier testing considered only when the family has exceptionally early cancer and results would change management.
  • Do not test relatives for a VUS as if it were a confirmed disease-causing variant.
  • Discuss preimplantation genetic testing for monogenic disease, prenatal diagnosis, donor gametes, adoption, and natural conception without testing in a non-directive manner.
  • For cleft lip/palate in the family, explain that this is uncommon but reported with CDH1.

Lifestyle, nutrition, and symptom safety-net

Patient summary

Lifestyle cannot remove inherited risk, but it supports general health and recovery. New swallowing trouble, persistent upper abdominal pain, repeated vomiting, early fullness, unexplained weight loss, or a new breast change needs prompt assessment rather than waiting for the next screening visit.

  • Avoid smoking and second-hand smoke. Limit alcohol according to Canadian low-risk guidance.
  • Use a balanced diet rich in vegetables, fruit, legumes, and whole grains, while limiting heavily salted, smoked, and processed meats.
  • Maintain regular activity and a healthy, stable weight without restrictive dieting.
  • Before gastrectomy, arrange dietitian education. After gastrectomy, use small frequent meals, separate liquids from meals if dumping occurs, prioritize protein, and replace micronutrients.
  • Symptoms do not reliably predict early diffuse gastric cancer. New concerning symptoms require expedited assessment even after a recent negative endoscopy.

Condition-specific care for an affected patient

Patient summary

If cancer develops, treatment is based on the cancer stage and tumour markers. The inherited CDH1 result also matters for surgical planning, family testing, and sometimes breast treatment choices.

For established diffuse gastric cancer, stage with standard multidisciplinary pathways. Somatic biomarkers may include HER2, mismatch repair/microsatellite instability, PD-L1, and CLDN18.2 according to disease setting and local standards. Immunotherapy is not used to prevent cancer in unaffected CDH1 carriers.

For lobular breast cancer, management follows stage, receptor status, HER2 status, genomic testing where appropriate, and extent of multifocal or bilateral disease. MRI may improve definition of extent. Germline CDH1 can influence discussion of bilateral surgery but does not by itself dictate systemic treatment.

Emerging and investigational prevention

Patient summary

Several research approaches may improve detection, but none replaces expert care today. These methods should be used in trials or specialist programs.

Trial-only or not yet standard

  • Probe-based confocal laser endomicroscopy and other enhanced imaging methods.
  • Artificial-intelligence-assisted endoscopic or pathology detection.
  • Circulating tumour DNA, gastric fluid biomarkers, or methylation assays for screening.
  • Risk models that combine family history, variant features, ancestry, and serial biopsy findings.
  • Drug prevention targeting E-cadherin-loss pathways.

Prospective surveillance cohorts suggest expert endoscopy can intercept some early lesions, but long-term non-inferiority to gastrectomy has not been established.

Summary of recommendations

Patient summary

The most important steps are confirming the variant, defining the family phenotype, arranging expert gastric care, and starting high-risk breast screening for women. A VUS is not treated like a pathogenic variant.

Classic CDH1-HDGC

Discuss prophylactic total gastrectomy in early adulthood. Use expert endoscopy before surgery and every 6-12 months if surgery is deferred.

Breast-predominant or incidental CDH1

Baseline expert endoscopy, careful pedigree review, and preference-sensitive discussion of surveillance versus surgery.

Women with CDH1 P/LP variant

Annual breast MRI from 30. Add mammography according to local high-risk guidance. Discuss mastectomy and endocrine prevention as options.

HDGC-like, gene-negative

Annual expert endoscopy for at least 2 years from 40 or 10 years before the earliest family case, minimum 18. Individualize thereafter.

CDH1 VUS

No variant-driven gastrectomy or mastectomy. Manage by personal and family history and seek reclassification.

Cross-cutting

Test and eradicate H. pylori, offer cascade testing, provide dietitian support, and reassess whenever guidelines or variant classification changes.

Comprendre la maladie liée à CDH1

En bref pour les patients

CDH1 est un gène suppresseur de tumeur. Un variant pathogène peut augmenter le risque de cancer gastrique diffus et, chez les femmes, de cancer du sein lobulaire. Ces cancers peuvent être difficiles à voir à l'imagerie ou à l'endoscopie courante. Le risque n'est pas identique dans toutes les familles. J'utilise donc le variant précis, la pathologie et l'histoire familiale pour guider les décisions.

Biologie. CDH1 code E-cadherin, a calcium-dependent cell-adhesion protein. La perte d'E-cadhérine disrupts epithelial cohesion and signalling. Tumorigenesis usually requires somatic inactivation of the remaining normal allele. Les foyers précurseurs gastriques peuvent être microscopiques, multifocaux et répartis sous une muqueuse d'apparence normale.

Phénotypes à distinguer.

  • Cancer gastrique diffus héréditaire classique lié à CDH1 : pathogenic or likely pathogenic variant with diffuse gastric cancer in the family.
  • Phénotype CDH1 à prédominance de cancer du sein lobulaire héréditaire : pathogenic variant with lobular breast cancer clustering but little or no diffuse gastric cancer.
  • Variant pathogène CDH1 découvert fortuitement : found through broad panel or secondary testing with limited phenotype information.
  • Famille de type HDGC sans variant identifié : clinical family criteria are met but no pathogenic CDH1 or CTNNA1 variant is found.
  • Variant CDH1 de signification incertaine : ne doit pas, à lui seul, mener à une gastrectomie ou à une surveillance à haut risque.

La transmission autosomique dominante signifie que chaque enfant d'une personne porteuse hétérozygote a 50 % de probabilité d'hériter du variant familial. La pénétrance est incomplète et fortement influencée par le mode d'identification de la famille et par l'histoire familiale.

Comment fonctionne cette voie

Les cellules de la muqueuse de l’estomac et du sein restent attachées grâce à une protéine « colle », l’E-cadhérine (produite par CDH1), ancrée à l’intérieur de la cellule par l’α-caténine (produite par CTNNA1). Quand cette colle est perdue, les cellules se détachent et peuvent se propager une à une dans le tissu, ce qui correspond au mode de croissance du cancer gastrique diffus et du cancer du sein lobulaire.

CDH1: CDH1 produit l’E-cadhérine elle-même, la colle entre cellules voisines.

Sources : Guilford P, et al. Nature 1998;392:402–5 (PubMed).

Chirurgie de réduction du risque et suivi postopératoire

En bref pour les patients

L'ablation complète de l'estomac prévient le cancer gastrique, mais entraîne des changements nutritionnels et digestifs permanents. Cette décision exige le soutien d'une équipe experte en chirurgie, nutrition, génétique, gastroentérologie, psychologie et pathologie. La chirurgie mammaire est optionnelle et dépend des préférences personnelles et de l'évaluation du risque.

Gastrectomie totale prophylactique

Use an experienced upper-GI surgeon and a centre with HDGC pathology expertise. The operation must remove all gastric mucosa, with confirmation of oesophageal and duodenal margins. Review oral drug absorption. Non-oral contraception may be preferable when reliable absorption is essential. Chimioprévention et prévention médicale

En bref pour les patients

Aucun médicament n'a démontré qu'il prévient le cancer gastrique lié à CDH1. Pour le risque mammaire, le tamoxifène ou une autre prévention endocrinienne peut aider certaines femmes, mais les données ne sont pas propres à CDH1. Nous évaluons les risques de thrombose, d'atteinte utérine, de perte osseuse et les effets ménopausiques avant de prescrire.

Gastrique : no validated chemoprevention. Eradicate H. pylori, avoid tobacco, and investigate persistent upper-GI symptoms promptly.

Conseil génétique et planification familiale

En bref pour les patients

Les proches parents devraient recevoir une offre de conseil génétique et de test pour le variant familial connu. Le test commence habituellement lorsque la prise en charge à l'âge adulte pourrait changer. Des options reproductives existent, mais le choix est personnel.

  • Construire un pedigree sur trois générations and verify diffuse gastric versus intestinal gastric histology and lobular versus ductal breast histology.
  • Offrir un dépistage familial ciblé to adult first-degree relatives. Each has a 50% chance of carrying the familial variant.
  • Le test est généralement offert from age 16-18 or legal adulthood, with earlier testing considered only when the family has exceptionally early cancer and results would change management.
  • Ne pas tester les proches pour un VUS as if it were a confirmed disease-causing variant.
  • Discuter du test génétique préimplantatoire for monogenic disease, prenatal diagnosis, donor gametes, adoption, and natural conception without testing in a non-directive manner.
  • For cleft lip/palate in the family, explain that this is uncommon but reported with CDH1.

7

Mode de vie, nutrition et signes d'alarme

En bref pour les patients

Le mode de vie ne peut pas éliminer le risque héréditaire, mais il favorise la santé générale et la récupération. Une nouvelle difficulté à avaler, une douleur persistante dans le haut de l'abdomen, des vomissements répétés, une satiété précoce, une perte de poids inexpliquée ou un nouveau changement mammaire exigent une évaluation rapide.

  • Éviter le tabagisme and second-hand smoke. Limit alcohol according to Canadian low-risk guidance.
  • Adopter une alimentation équilibrée rich in vegetables, fruit, legumes, and whole grains, while limiting heavily salted, smoked, and processed meats.
  • Maintenir une activité régulière and a healthy, stable weight without restrictive dieting.
  • Avant la gastrectomie, arrange dietitian education. After gastrectomy, use small frequent meals, separate liquids from meals if dumping occurs, prioritize protein, and replace micronutrients.
  • Les symptômes ne prédisent pas de façon fiable early diffuse gastric cancer. New concerning symptoms require expedited assessment even after a recent negative endoscopy.
8

Soins propres à une personne atteinte

En bref pour les patients

Si un cancer apparaît, le traitement dépend du stade et des marqueurs tumoraux. Le résultat germinal CDH1 est aussi pertinent pour la planification chirurgicale, le dépistage familial et parfois les choix de traitement mammaire.

Pour un cancer gastrique diffus établi, stage with standard multidisciplinary pathways. Somatic biomarkers may include HER2, mismatch repair/microsatellite instability, PD-L1, and CLDN18.2 according to disease setting and local standards. Immunotherapy is not used to prevent cancer in unaffected CDH1 carriers.

Pour un cancer du sein lobulaire, management follows stage, receptor status, HER2 status, genomic testing where appropriate, and extent of multifocal or bilateral disease. MRI may improve definition of extent. Germline CDH1 can influence discussion of bilateral surgery but does not by itself dictate systemic treatment.

9

Prévention émergente et expérimentale

En bref pour les patients

Plusieurs approches de recherche pourraient améliorer la détection, mais aucune ne remplace les soins experts aujourd'hui. Elles devraient être utilisées dans le cadre d'essais ou de programmes spécialisés.

Réservé aux essais ou non standard

  • Probe-based confocal laser endomicroscopy and other enhanced imaging methods.
  • Artificial-intelligence-assisted endoscopic or pathology detection.
  • Circulating tumour DNA, gastric fluid biomarkers, or methylation assays for screening.
  • Risk models that combine family history, variant features, ancestry, and serial biopsy findings.
  • Drug prevention targeting E-cadherin-loss pathways.

Les cohortes prospectives de surveillance suggèrent expert endoscopy can intercept some early lesions, but long-term non-inferiority to gastrectomy has not been established.

Résumé des recommandations

En bref pour les patients

Les étapes les plus importantes sont de confirmer le variant, de définir le phénotype familial, d'organiser des soins gastriques experts et de commencer la surveillance mammaire à haut risque chez les femmes. Un VUS ne se traite pas comme un variant pathogène.

CDH1-HDGC classique

Discuss prophylactic total gastrectomy in early adulthood. Use expert endoscopy before surgery and every 6-12 months if surgery is deferred.

CDH1 à prédominance mammaire ou découvert fortuitement

Baseline expert endoscopy, careful pedigree review, and preference-sensitive discussion of surveillance versus surgery.

Femmes avec variant CDH1 P/LP

Annual breast MRI from 30. Add mammography according to local high-risk guidance. Discuss mastectomy and endocrine prevention as options.

HDGC-like sans variant identifié

Annual expert endoscopy for at least 2 years from 40 or 10 years before the earliest family case, minimum 18. Individualize thereafter.

CDH1 VUS

No variant-driven gastrectomy or mastectomy. Manage by personal and family history and seek reclassification.

Mesures transversales

Test and eradicate H. pylori, offer cascade testing, provide dietitian support, and reassess whenever guidelines or variant classification changes.
